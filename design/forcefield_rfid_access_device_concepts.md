# Force-Field Door Hub: 4 Integrated Concepts (Vance, v2, Oct 28 2026)
Supersedes the 5 scanner-only concepts (kept as forcefield_rfid_access_device_concepts_v1_scanner_only.md).
Brief (Jared): a futuristic O'Neill-cylinder resident door hub that combines the unit number, a Sprocket dock/service port, a display, and the wrist [F] RFID scan, plus anything else a real habitat would need.

## Shared rules (all 4)
- Mounts on the +Z jamb side of the v2 frame and shares its stepped pearl (#D8DEE6) / graphite (#282E38) bezel. Nothing more than 5 mm proud of the wall face (wall 0.30 m deep).
- States: standby amber #FF9500 (restrained); granted #3FAF6A; denied = two amber blinks. On grant, light runs from the hub into the frame's conduit light pipe and up to the corner emitters, and the violet field drops.
- Unit number: crisp etched/inlaid texture (e.g. "C-214"); never floating text.
- Display: matte low-glare glass screen showing pictograms only (wordless teaching): occupant-present dot, field state, pressure/O2 bars, message envelope, spin/gravity glyph.
- Sprocket dock: at floor level so the treaded bot backs in. A data/charge coupler sits at bot-head height; a service conduit runs inside the frame to the field emitters. At the dock the bot does three things: (1) tops up charge by induction, (2) syncs the door's log and maintenance flags, (3) runs emitter calibration and dust purge through the conduit (visible as a light pulse travelling up the frame pipe). An occupied dock reads as a lit coupler ring.
- Intercom: fine perforated grille with a small press-to-talk pad, cyan #00E5FF ring while open.
- Emergency: signal-orange #D95B26 manual field-drop lever behind a hinged cover on every hub (habitat code).

## 1. Threshold Spine (full-height jamb pilaster)
- Layout: a slim vertical pilaster that runs the full height of the jamb. Top to bottom: (a) etched unit-number plate at 1.85 m; (b) a small round spin-lock gyro window (from Spin Governor) at 1.65 m; (c) a portrait display 0.14 x 0.20 at eye level (1.45 m); (d) Tendon Harp reed scanner window 0.12 x 0.16 at 1.15 m; (e) intercom grille plus orange override cover at 0.95 m; (f) Sprocket berth at the base.
- Pocket: 0.20 W x 2.10 H x 0.18 D; base berth 0.20 W x 0.35 H, recessed 0.25 D.
- Sprocket docking: the bot backs its tail into the base berth onto a floor induction plate; a coupler pin meets its rear deck. Calibration pulses run straight up the pilaster's light pipe into the frame.
- States: Standby: a thin amber line along the pilaster edge, display dim, reeds still. Scanning: the reed wave mirrors the sleeve pulse, amber climbs the pilaster line, and the gyro tick hunts for its notch. Granted: the gyro locks, reeds settle with green tips, the pilaster line turns green bottom to top and feeds the frame.
- Silhouette: a tall, narrow, architectural column that reads as part of the doorframe; the cleanest residential option.

## 2. Halo Panel (compact eye-level hub + toe-kick dock)
- Layout: a square eye-level panel. The Petal Iris scanner (from concept #4) sits at the center, wrapped by a thin ring display: radial segments show field state, pressure, and messages around the iris. The unit number is on a stepped pearl tab above. Two slim pressure sight columns (from Pressure Lung) flank the panel, one per side of the door.
- Pocket: panel 0.32 W x 0.40 H x 0.12 D at 1.25 m center; separate toe-kick dock 0.40 W x 0.25 H x 0.25 D under the jamb.
- Sprocket docking: drives nose-in under the jamb; its Hammerhead stereo-bar head nests into a horizontal coupler slot. Purely low-profile, so it doesn't block traffic.
- States: Standby: petals closed, seams amber, the ring shows a slow amber sweep. Scanning: the iris opens, the cup slides forward 8 mm, and the ring segments fill clockwise. Granted: the ring turns fully green and the petals half-close, leaving a lit green hex rim while the field is down. Unsafe pressure keeps the columns mismatched and the ring amber.
- Silhouette: a round-in-square face with stepped bezels; the most iconic and readable at distance.

## 3. Service Niche (hub + full Sprocket bay, utility corridors)
- Layout: a recessed alcove beside the door. Upper part: landscape display 0.30 x 0.18, scanner pad below it, and the unit number with the district emblem as a Lenticular Emblem Stack (from concept #5) that resolves on grant. Lower part: a Sprocket berth deep enough to park the whole bot flush with the wall, with a tool/consumable shelf (Super Wire, Thermal Jelly canisters) above it.
- Pocket: 0.55 W x 1.50 H x 0.28 D (uses nearly the full wall depth; place where the wall allows).
- Sprocket docking: the bot reverses fully in; the coupler arm drops onto its back. When docked it can extend a probe up into the conduit to service the emitter, giving the player a visible "maintenance mode" (ties to Mechanic B co-op).
- States: Standby: emblem slats scrambled, amber edge light, the bay rim a soft amber line. Scanning: slats rotate into line bottom to top while the display bars fill. Granted: the emblem and unit number resolve crisp, edge light green, frame pipe pulses green to the emitters.
- Silhouette: a deep, utilitarian niche; strongest for Engineering (D-01) and maintenance corridors.

## 4. Bulkhead Hub (sector and pressure-boundary doors)
- Layout: a ruggedized version for doors between pressure zones or sectors. Large display 0.24 x 0.30 (pressure on both sides, O2, gravity glyph). Pressure Lung membrane and twin columns. Scanner pad. Intercom. A larger signal-orange manual override lever behind a breakable cover. Unit or sector number in large stenciled texture. A Sprocket port sits low on the jamb with a sealed flap.
- Pocket: 0.40 W x 0.80 H x 0.20 D at 1.0–1.8 m, plus a 0.35 W x 0.30 H flapped dock at the floor.
- Sprocket docking: nose-in under the flap; on these doors its job is emitter calibration and a pressure-seal check before the field can drop.
- States: Standby: the membrane breathes slowly with amber menisci. Scanning: the membrane holds and the columns equalize. Granted: the columns level at a green band, the display goes green, and the field drops. Hazard: it stays amber even with a valid wrist, and only the orange override (with an alarm state) drops the field. This is a natural Chapter 1 "Solar Flare" set piece.
- Silhouette: chunky, armored, visibly industrial; signals "this door matters."

## Recommendation
Use #1 Threshold Spine for residential units, #3 Service Niche for maintenance corridors, and #4 Bulkhead Hub for pressure boundaries: one family with a shared kit (scanner, display, coupler, override). #2 Halo Panel is the best single all-purpose option if only one is modelled.
